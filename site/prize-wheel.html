How to play

The wheel has 12 equal sectors, so each is equally likely to land under the pointer. The number in a sector is the prize it pays, from 0 to 10. A spin wins if its prize reaches the bar, so your chance of winning is the number of sectors that reach it, out of 12.

Shape the prizes with two sliders: the big prize, and how many sectors pay it. The other sectors share whatever is left, so the average never changes.

Best possible is the most sectors any wheel could win in this round. Change the wheel, then spin. The verdict judges its chance of winning, even when that one spin loses.

The idea behind the game

Same average, different chances

The average prize stays fixed, but the chance of reaching the bar changes. A bigger top prize can leave fewer sectors reaching the bar. A wheel that works well for one bar can work poorly for another.

The average is the expected value of a spin. This unit studies the full distribution of possible prizes and how to summarize its spread with variance. Neither the average alone nor one lucky spin tells you the chance of winning.

Every sector is equally likely. The winning chance is counted exactly; a random spin illustrates that chance and never decides the verdict. New rounds change the average, the bar and the starting wheel while keeping the same two controls.
