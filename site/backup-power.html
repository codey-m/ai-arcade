How to play

The harbor needs enough lamps lit tonight for boats to find the entrance. The goal is a plan that keeps it lit on at least 9 nights in 10, for as little as possible.

Generators power the lamps. A generator that fails to start leaves every lamp on it dark. The weather sets how often generators start.

Lamps can also burn out on their own, even on a running generator.

Plan the wiring by dragging lamps: from one generator to another, out of the crate of spare lamps, back into it, or onto the empty slot to start a new generator. You can also press a lamp, then press where it goes. Generators cost more than lamps.

Run the night to watch one night play out and learn whether your plan keeps the harbor lit on at least 9 nights in 10. Par, the cost of the cheapest reliable plan, appears once you find a reliable plan. New night brings new weather, a new need and new prices.

The idea behind the game

Lamps on one generator go dark together

Every lamp is lit on the same share of nights however you wire it. The wiring changes which lamps fail together. Lamps on one generator share its start, so adding lamps to one generator can never make the harbor more reliable than that generator. On a calm night that is fine, and it is the cheapest plan. On a stormy night the harbor needs lamps whose failures don't travel together.

Treating each lamp as its own separate chance is only right when nothing links them. That is the idea of independence in Probability & Statistical Data Analysis (6.3710.1x) Unit 2, where systems like this one work only if enough of their parts do.

The verdict does not come from the night shown. Each plan has an exact chance, found by adding up every combination of generators starting and, for each one, the chance that enough bulbs light. Each generator's start and each bulb are separate chances that nothing else affects. The night shown is one simulated night; run it again to see others. The par is the cheapest plan that keeps the harbor lit on at least 90 nights in 100, found over every way to wire up to four generators with up to four lamps each. The exact chance stays hidden: a run says only whether the plan reaches 9 nights in 10, so the game is about reasoning, not reading a number.
