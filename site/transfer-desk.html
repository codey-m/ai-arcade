            How to play

            Inspect the requests and receipts, choose a repeat rule, then run the incident in a test copy. A passing test lets you install the rule before reconciling the live account. Reading and changing a selection cost no tests, and there is no timer.

            On the test bench, keys 1-3 select a rule. Tab moves between controls; Enter or Space activates the focused control. Playback can be skipped.

              The idea behind the game

              Why the repeat rule matters

              The desk has to distinguish a repeated delivery from another payment. A customer’s name or a standing-order reference can belong to several requests. The rule has to preserve the boundary of the payment the customer actually asked for.

              The assistant’s confirmation cannot establish which transfers belong in the account. Protected receipts record the executions. Installing a repair changes future decisions; reconciling the account corrects extra executions or completes requests that are still pending.

              Ana made two requests, and the app delivered one of them twice. The repeat rule protected her money while still letting her next request through. A separate reversal corrected the extra debit.

              In an AI system, a requirement needs a mechanism that enforces the right distinction. These incidents model one small desk and the requests shown on it.

              What the desk has to guarantee

              A retry can arrive after a confirmation is lost, while a standing instruction can generate a fresh payment next month. The repeat check must tell those situations apart and admit each legitimate payment.

              The desk also has to check the money available when a request arrives, confirm who may spend it, and make the debit and credit together. It can count a payment as done only after that move succeeds. Each part enforces a different requirement.

              Protected receipts supply evidence of what actually happened. Installing a repair changes future decisions; reconciliation corrects the recorded damage. Replaying earlier incidents checks that a new repair preserves what already worked.

              In an AI system, a stated requirement needs a mechanism that enforces it and tests that expose failures. These incidents model the requests and accounts shown on this desk.
